# Iron & Gold — Stage 6 (Events, Formable Nations and the 1776 Start) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:**
- **Events:** history happens as event cards with 2–3 choices, fired by date or conditions, for the player and the AI alike.
- **Independence revolts and formable nations:** the American Revolution, the Confederacy, Germany and Italy.
- **A second start date, 1776 to 1876,** with its own world and event chains.

**Architecture:**
- **Events are data.** `src/content/events/*.ts` holds `EventDef`s built from a small set of conditions and effects. `src/sim/events.ts` evaluates the triggers once a month (cheap: each event names the countries it can fire for). AI countries choose at once by option weight; the player gets a pending event, answered with the `chooseEventOption` command or defaulted after 30 days.
- **Effects reuse existing systems:** laws (`setLaw`), revolts (`startRevolt` with an independence kind), plays (`startPlay`), relations, money, techs, temporary modifiers through `effectSources`, and forming nations.
- **Formable nations are data** (`src/content/formables.ts`) behind a `formNation` command.
- **The 1776 start** is a second `StartDef` alongside 1836.

**Tech Stack:** unchanged.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-17-iron-and-gold-design.md`, sections "Events", "Sessions and pacing" (start dates), "Laws and politics" (revolts: "Civil wars and the American Revolution use this system") and Interface ("Event notifications appear as a toast at the top; tapping opens the event card").

## Global Constraints

- All earlier Global Constraints apply (determinism, commands, content as data, iOS UI rules, perf budget).
- **Event card:** a title, 2–4 short paragraphs, and 2–3 options, each with its effects listed in plain words ("+£50k", "Prussia: relations −30", "Rebels rise in 13 states"). Chains carry on through flags.
- **Triggers:** a date window, conditions, and a mean time to happen in months (deterministic through the seeded RNG). Most events fire once per game.
- **AI choice:** weighted by option `ai` weights. The historical option weighs most on easier difficulties (history plays out); on Hard and above the AI weighs its own interest a little more (it still picks by weight).
- **No surprise attacks:** events can start diplomatic plays or revolts, never a war out of nothing.
- **Independence revolts:** the rebels carry a tag and name (e.g. USA "United States"). If they win, they stay a real country and keep their states. If they lose, the land goes back.
- **Formable nations:** Germany (from Prussia or Austria, needing most of the German states), Italy (from Sardinia, Sicily or the Papal States, needing most of the Italian states), and more later. Forming changes the tag, name and colour, keeps everything else, and gives a modest bonus.
- **1776 start:** at least 55 countries. The 13 colonies are British, Poland-Lithuania exists, the Holy Roman Empire is split into states, the Mughal Empire has broken up (Marathas, Mysore, Hyderabad, Bengal under the Company), Spain and Portugal own the Americas, and Qing is at its height. Laws, techs and personalities are set; the game ends in 1876.
- Saves: `SAVE_VERSION` 6.

## Review Focus

1. **An event for a country that no longer exists, or whose conditions stop holding** while the player is deciding → the choice still applies safely; effects that make no sense now are skipped and the card says so (test in Task 1).
2. **An independence revolt while the parent is at war, a puppet, or in a bloc**; and rebels that win and later lose their only state → a consistent map and wars (test in Task 2).
3. **Forming Germany while in a bloc, a puppet, mid-war, or as the player** → the tag change keeps relations, treaties, bloc, puppets and wars; labels update (test in Task 2).
4. **Many events firing in one month** on a 1776 or 1836 world → the bench stays within budget, and the player never gets more than 2 cards at once; the rest queue (test in Task 3).
5. **The 1776 world:** every country has a valid capital, populations are plausible (no Liechtenstein at 20M), and it saves, loads and runs 10 years with no rejected AI commands (test in Task 5).

### Task 1: Event engine
`src/content/events/types.ts` defines:
- `EventDef { id, title, text, countries: string[] | 'any', start?: GameDate, end?: GameDate, mtthMonths, conditions: Condition[], options: EventOption[], repeat?: false }`
- `Condition` (year, hasTech, law, ownsState(key), ownsAll(keys), controlsShare(prefix, share), atWarWith(tag), exists(tag), flag, notFlag, relationBelow/Above(tag, v), isAI)
- `EventOption { label, effects: EventEffect[], ai: number }`
- `EventEffect` (money, setLaw, relation(tag, delta), flag, clearFlag, modifier(effects, months, name), tech, troops, startPlay(target tag, demands), transferStates(keys, to tag), revolt(group, independence?: { tag, name, color, states: keys }), formNation(tag), stability(group approval ±))

`GameState.flags: string[]`, `Country.flags`, `Country.modifiers`, and `GameState.pendingEvents: { id, country, tick }[]`.

`src/sim/events.ts` runs monthly. For each event, for each country it could fire for: check the date window and conditions, then fire with chance `1 / mtth` (or at once when mtth is 0). The AI picks by weight and effects apply at once. The player gets a pending card: at most 2 open, the rest wait. The `chooseEventOption` command applies the choice, and after 30 days option 0 is applied automatically. Effects that no longer make sense are skipped.

Tests: date and conditions, mtth determinism, AI choice, player choice and timeout, stale effects skipped (Review Focus 1), modifiers expire.

### Task 2: Independence revolts, released nations, formable nations
- `startRevolt` gains an `independence` kind: the rebels get a tag and name and the war goal `{ kind: 'independence' }` (a new Demand priced 70). If they win, `rebel` is cleared and they become a normal country. If they lose, they dissolve as now.
- Effect `release(tag, name, color, states)` creates a country with no war.
- `src/content/formables.ts`: `FormableDef { tag, name, color, from: string[] (tags), cores: string[] (state-key prefixes or keys), share, capital }`.
- Command `formNation {tag}`, checked by `formCheck`: an eligible tag, owning ≥ `share` of the cores, not a puppet, not at war. It changes the tag, name and colour, adds `+5% output` as a permanent modifier, and annexes the country's puppets inside the cores. AI countries form when they can.

Tests (Review Focus 2, 3).

### Task 3: Event cards in the UI
The worker sends `events` (the player's pending cards with option effect texts). An event card sheet (modal over the map) shows the title, text and options with effects, at ≥ 44 px. A toast "New event: …" opens the card when tapped. A Nation tab section, "Decisions", lists formable nations with progress ("own 14 of 18 German states") and a Form button. History: the last 20 events the player saw.

Tests: component tests and an e2e that forces an event via the debug hook `__fireEvent(id)`, chooses, and sees the effect. Review Focus 4 (queue of 2).

### Task 4: 1836 events and formables
- **Great Game** (GBR/RUS, 1836–1890): a chain of three events over Afghanistan and Central Asia. Each offers relations, plays against AFG/KHI/BUK, or a détente.
- **German unification** (PRU): Zollverein (founds or expands its bloc), the Danish question (a play on Schleswig-Holstein), the Austrian question (a play or war over leadership), then Germany is proclaimed (form Germany).
- **Italian unification** (SAR): Risorgimento, Garibaldi (Sicily rebels join), Rome. Form Italy.
- **Opening of Japan** (JAP, 1853–1860): Perry arrives. Open up (free trade, techs, relations) or resist (a play from the USA).
- **Opium Wars** (GBR and CHI, 1839–1860): GBR's play for an open market and Hong Kong; CHI chooses to ban opium (relations) or legalise it.
- **Eastern Question** (TUR): Greek and Serbian unrest (independence revolts), Egyptian ambitions, and Russia's Black Sea interest.
- **US westward expansion** (USA): Texas annexation (TEX joins if it agrees), Oregon, the Mexican cession (a play on California).
- **US Civil War** (USA, 1858–1865, slavery law still in force): the southern states rise as the Confederate States (independence). On a USA win, a free labour law.

Formables: Germany, Italy.

Tests: every event refers to existing tags, states, techs and laws, and every option has effects. A 30-year 1836 probe shows events firing for the right countries at plausible dates.

### Task 5: The 1776 start
- `src/content/starts/1776/{countries,ownership,population,techs,laws}.ts`: about 60 countries with personalities.
- Ownership by modern-territory rules, as in 1836, with state-level overrides (the 13 colonies, Poland-Lithuania, Quebec, the Spanish and Portuguese Americas, Mughal successors). Populations come from 1776 estimates.
- Starting techs: no era 1 techs except a few for advanced countries. Laws are mostly absolute monarchy and serfdom in the east.

1776 events:
- **American Revolution** (GBR, 1775–1783): the colonies rise as the USA (independence revolt), and France may back them.
- **French Revolution** (FRA, 1787–1795, triggered by debt or radical groups): a republic, the Terror, then plays against Austria and Prussia.
- **Napoleonic chain** (FRA, 1799–1815): First Consul, Emperor (military modifiers), the Continental System (an embargo play against GBR), the Russian campaign.
- **Partitions of Poland** (RUS/PRU/AUS, 1772–1795): transfers of Polish states.

Tests (Review Focus 5): the world builds, capitals are valid, populations sit in plausible ranges, 10 AI years run with no rejected commands, and save/load works.
